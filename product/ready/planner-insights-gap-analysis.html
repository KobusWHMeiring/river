Three Patterns We Noticed

① Tasks are planned but rarely completed.
Only 29 of 316 planned tasks were ever marked done. 133 days had planned tasks on the calendar but zero visit logs were created on those days. The planner is being used as a wishlist, not a work-tracking tool.
② Almost all logging goes through tasks — but that adds friction.
28 of 30 visit logs are linked to a pre-existing task. Only 2 logs were created "unplanned" from the New Log button. This means the team is creating tasks just to log work, or not logging at all if there’s no task. The Quick Log feature (already on the backlog) could help.
③ Admin dominates the planner, field work is underrepresented.
In August: 10 admin tasks vs 3 weeding, 2 planting, 1 litter run. In July: 16 admin vs 19 field tasks combined. Either admin genuinely consumes most time, or field tasks aren’t being entered into the planner.

Proposed Ideas to Address the Gaps

These are not commitments — they’re starting points to discuss. Which of these would actually change how the team uses the app?

Visibility

A. Planner Health Indicator

Add a simple counter at the top of the planner and dashboard: "This week: 8 tasks planned, 2 completed, 3 logs recorded." Makes the planning-to-execution gap visible without changing how anyone works. No new workflow — just a number.

Visibility

B. Section Activity Staleness

On the sections list and dashboard, show when each section was last active: "Rosebank — last activity 22 July". Sections with no recent activity get a subtle amber highlight. Observatory Wetland with 7 planned, 0 done would be immediately visible.

Visibility

C. Overdue Task Highlighting

Tasks past their date that are still incomplete get a red/amber tint in the planner. A task from July sitting incomplete in August is invisible right now. This creates visual urgency without nagging.

Reduces Friction

D. Quick Log from Planner (Jess’s request)

The New Log button already exists on the planner, but unplanned logs are rare (2 of 30). Should the Quick Log flow be simpler? Pre-fill the date from the planner view, skip the task selection step, and let the team log field work in one click.

Reduces Friction

E. Tick-to-Complete on Planner (Your request #3)

Complete a task directly from the planner with one click — no need to open a form, create a visit log, and mark it complete through three steps. A single ✓ on the planner card marks it done.

Questions for You

1. Is the planner the right fit?

With 9.2% completion, we’re wondering: is the weekly/monthly planner the right way to manage the team’s work? Or does the way work actually happens (ad-hoc, weather-dependent, volunteer-driven) not fit a pre-planned calendar model? Would a simpler daily log-first approach work better — where field work is recorded after it happens, and the planner is secondary?

2. What’s happening with admin tasks?

59% of August’s planned tasks are admin. Is that accurate — is admin genuinely half the workload? Or are admin tasks easier to plan (they have fixed dates) while field tasks happen spontaneously and don’t make it onto the planner?

3. Which friction point matters most?

We’ve identified two types of friction: (a) the planner doesn’t reflect reality — tasks are planned but not done, and (b) logging completed work takes too many steps. Would fixing the logging flow (Quick Log, tick-to-complete) actually increase how much work gets logged? Or is the real issue that work happens differently than how the planner expects?

4. Do you want more visibility, or less friction?

Items A–C are pure visibility — they surface what’s already in the data. Items D–E change how people interact with the app. Which direction should we invest in first? Or should we do one of each?
